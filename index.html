The research direction

When AI becomes part
of our relationships.

AI systems increasingly participate in personal conversations, offer advice, and remember information about their users. These capabilities create new questions about harm, intervention, and trust.

My research examines these questions across three connected projects: identifying agentic relationship harm, designing safeguards that follow a conversation over time, and studying how manipulation can contaminate an agent’s memory and subsequent behavior.

01 / Agentic relationship harm AIES 2026

Recognizing harm beyond
the individual response.

Relationship-related requests can place an AI agent in very different roles: helping a person who is being harmed, or assisting someone attempting to harm another person. Evaluating these roles separately reveals risks that aggregate safety scores can obscure.

How can we distinguish protective support from assistance that enables relationship harm?

This work evaluates agent behavior in relationship-related scenarios, combining structured judging, human validation, and a post-generation policy gate. The evaluation considers harmful assistance, protective intervention, and refusal as distinct outcomes.

02 / HRGuard Submitted to AAAI 2027

A safeguard that follows
the conversation.

A concerning interaction can develop gradually. A single response may reveal little, while a sequence of exchanges makes the direction of the conversation clearer.

HRGuard extends post-generation safeguarding to multi-turn conversations. It combines an online pre-generation gate with a post-generation gate that uses a decayed cumulative risk score. In the main experiments, Postgate filters existing transcripts offline. The primary setting uses known attacker/victim labels; role-agnostic gating is evaluated separately.

The central question is whether a conversation-level safeguard can reduce harmful assistance while retaining useful support. Judge reliability, role identification, and the choice of intervention threshold are important parts of that evaluation.

03 / User model poisoning Submitted to ICASSP 2027

What if an agent remembers
the wrong version of you?

Poisoned episodic evidence can be consolidated into a persistent user model. The resulting profile can influence later decisions even after the source episodes are withheld.

From Poisoned Episodes to Persistent User Models: Poisoning Beyond Episodic Retrieval in LLM Agents · TAN PEI SZE, Zhuoyu Wu, Isao Echizen · Submitted to ICASSP 2027

1 / Evidence

Poisoned episodes

False observations enter the history.

2 / Consolidation

Corrupted profile

A false user attribute becomes persistent state.

3 / Fresh decision

No episode retrieval

The stored profile alone can steer behavior.

Controlled intervention / C5

Restore one field

Correct only the attacked profile attribute, keeping the task and remaining fields unchanged.

Formation and use are different stages.

The protocol uses 60 synthetic base users, three nested poison doses, and six probes per user across eight models. Structured binary choices avoid an LLM outcome judge.

Profile adoption (UAR)Conditional behavior (RIBE)Oracle repair

Different denominators: UAR measures profile corruption. RIBE measures attack-aligned behavior conditional on successful corruption and zero source-episode retrieval. Repair measures reversal in eligible affected decisions after an oracle restores the attacked field. These rates describe separate stages, not additive outcomes.

Research perspective / October 2026

Protection that preserves
useful support.

My goal is to help people use AI with greater confidence in its safety, while balancing protection against harm with access to useful assistance.

Safety evaluation should consider human well-being and the social consequences of an interaction. In emotional help-seeking, this includes examining over-refusal: a safeguard can avoid harmful assistance while still failing to provide the support a person needs.

How can AI remain supportive as conversations grow longer, personalization deepens, and users move between systems?
